# Diecut

Home-screen widgets cut to any shape.

Android widgets are almost always rounded rectangles. Diecut makes widgets of your
own things (a photo, a clock, the apps you open most) in shapes a launcher never
offers: a postage stamp with real perforations, a photo frame, a circle, a ticket,
a polaroid. Where a shape cuts in, the wallpaper shows through.

A die cut is the printer's word for cutting paper into a shape.

## Version 1

**Content**
- **Photo**: one picture, or a few that take turns.
- **Clock**: digital or analogue, in the shape.
- **App shortcuts**: one app, or a small group, each a tap away.
- Maybe later: a date, or a short note.

**Shapes**
- Postage stamp: perforated edges, a hole at each corner (proven: see below)
- Photo frame: a mat with a window, or a polaroid with a deep bottom edge
- Circle and squircle
- Ticket: notches cut into both sides
- Scalloped (cookie-cutter) edge
- Arch
- Later, maybe: film frame, torn-paper edge, speech bubble, hexagon

Each widget picks its content, its shape and its colours when it is added, and can be
changed later.

**Not in version 1:** reshaping other apps' widgets. It can be done (Diecut would host
the other widget out of sight, snapshot it and cut the snapshot), but taps become one
tap, lists don't scroll, nothing animates, and keeping it current costs battery. It
could come later as "Frame another widget (beta)". Fully live reshaped widgets would
need Diecut to be the launcher itself, which is a separate project.

## Where this came from

The postage-stamp widget in [TomeVoice](https://github.com/Sayandeep1013/TomeVoice)
(the "Now reading (stamp)" widget, added in 1.0.6) proved the technique on a Nothing
Phone. See `CLAUDE.md` for how it works and how to start.
